What we handle

The jobs that fill our phone

Most calls fall into a few groups. Drains and sewer lines come first: a kitchen sink that will not clear, a tub that backs up or a floor drain that gurgles. Drain cleaning handles the everyday cases, hydro jetting handles grease and roots, and sewer line repair covers a main that has failed.

Water heaters are the next group. A tank that leaks or a heater that has gone cold calls for water heater repair, or for replacement when the unit is worn out. Planned work, such as tankless installs, repiping and softener and filtration work, is scheduled around your week instead of your emergency.

Leaks make up the third group, and they are often the hardest to see. A meter that turns with every fixture off, a warm patch on a floor or a stain that keeps returning points to leak detection. Businesses call about commercial plumbing after hours, when a restaurant or shop cannot wait until morning.

Dana Point

Why local knowledge matters for plumbing

Dana Point was incorporated on January 1, 1989, and it was joined that year by Capistrano Beach, which has its own ZIP code, 92624. Housing near the harbor, in Lantern Village and across the hillside neighborhoods covers many decades, and pipe ages with the house. A repair that suits one block can be wrong for the next.

Water and wastewater service in Dana Point comes from South Coast Water District, which says its supply is imported through the Municipal Water District of Orange County and Metropolitan, with local groundwater and recycled water added. When a problem sits on the street side of the meter, the district is the right call. When it sits on the house side, we are.

The City of Dana Point's Building and Safety Division handles permits and inspections, and it says permits are generally required for almost all construction. We tell customers when a job is likely to need one and who to ask. The division's main number is (949) 248-3564.

Our standard

How we decide what to recommend

A good plumber starts by asking what the problem actually is. A drain that is slow in one fixture is a different job from a drain that is slow in every fixture, and a heater that is leaking at a fitting is a different job from a heater that is leaking at the tank. We ask those questions on the phone so the first price you hear is a realistic one.

If a repair will hold, we recommend the repair. If a part is at the end of its life, we say so and explain why. The choice between repair and replacement is yours, and you hear the price of each before work starts. Landlords, property managers and owners who live out of town can authorize work by phone and ask for written findings and photos afterward.

We do not use contact forms, and we do not promise arrival times we cannot keep. When you call, you hear when a plumber can be there, and if that changes, you hear it from us. Same-day appointments are available when schedules allow, and after-hours calls are answered the same way as daytime ones.
