About the area

What Lantern Village is, and where it sits

The Dana Point Historical Society describes its self-guided walking tour as covering the oldest and most attractive section of Dana Point. The tour is under two miles long, has 18 stops and starts at Golden Lantern and Del Prado.

The lantern names are no accident. The Historical Society says developer Anna Walters named Green Lantern in 1925, the year her home was finished. It adds that Sidney H. Woodruff's Dana Point Syndicate promoted the nautical theme and installed lantern street lights from 1926 to 1930.

Woodruff, a Los Angeles builder, bought about 900 acres and planned what the Society calls a Mediterranean village by the sea. The Depression ended his plans, and the society says development did not resume until the 1960s.

Housing mix

Why plumbing here is not one-size-fits-all

Local real estate guides describe a mix in this district: older cottages and established homes, condos, townhomes and some newer mixed-use buildings. We cannot tell you how old the pipe is in a given house from the street name alone, because remodels over the years change what is behind the walls.

That is why a sewer camera inspection answers more than a guess about the home's age. A camera shows the actual line, its joints and any root intrusion, and it turns a vague worry into a specific repair or a clear all-clear.

A house that has been remodeled in stages can hold several generations of pipe behind one wall. If low pressure or discolored water is a repeat problem, ask a plumber to check the supply lines and the shutoff valves before deciding whether repiping makes sense.

Common calls

Plumbing calls that fit a walkable, mixed-use neighborhood

These are the kinds of problems that tend to come up where homes, condos and small businesses sit close together. They are general plumbing patterns, not claims about any one street.

Condos and townhomes

Know which shutoff valve is yours and which belongs to the building. When a leak is in a shared wall, tell the property manager as well as the plumber.

Restaurants and cafes

Kitchens send grease down the drain. Repeat blockages point to hydro jetting and a regular cleaning schedule.

Older kitchens and baths

Original shutoff valves can seize. Replace them during any faucet repair so they close when you need them.

Water heaters in tight closets

Local note

Permits and who to call for what

Many plumbing jobs that change pipe or fixtures need a permit. The City of Dana Point's Building and Safety Division, at (949) 248-3564, can say whether yours does. Ask the plumber to name who pulls the permit and when the inspection happens. Plumbing work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is Part 5 of Title 24 and is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code. According to the City of Dana Point, permits are generally required for almost all construction.

Water and sewer service in much of Dana Point comes from South Coast Water District. If water is running in the street, call the district. If it is running inside the property line, call us. Your water bill shows which utility serves your address.
