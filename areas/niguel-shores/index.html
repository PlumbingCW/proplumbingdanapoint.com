About the area

What Niguel Shores is, and how access works

The Niguel Shores Community Association, which runs the community, lists its address as 33654 Niguel Shores Drive in Dana Point. Its website says the Mariner Gatehouse at 33510 Mariner Drive is where guests and service personnel must enter to reach the main section of the community.

Wikipedia notes that the community of Niguel Shores is subject to the eroding bluffs of the Strand. Local real estate guides describe the community as developed in the 1970s, with single-story homes and larger residences. Treat the age as background, not a fact about any one house.

The association also publishes safety notices for beach access at Niguel Shores and Breakers Isle, which shows how closely the community sits to the coast.

Bluffs and leaks

Why a leak near a slope can be hard to find

Water that escapes from a pipe near a slope does not always rise where the pipe is. It can move along soil or under a slab and surface farther away, and a patch of damp ground may be several feet from the failure.

That is why acoustic and thermal leak detection beats digging at random. If the tools point to a line under the floor, slab leak repair is the next conversation. If the line is outside the house, water service line repair covers the run from the meter.

Soil movement near a bluff can also stress a drain. If a toilet or tub drains slowly after rain, ask about a camera inspection. A camera shows whether a joint has shifted before it becomes a backup.

Local note

Permits, utilities and the neighbors next door

For a repair that changes pipe or fixtures, ask the plumber whether a permit is needed. The City of Dana Point's Building and Safety Division, at (949) 248-3564, is the office to ask. Plumbing work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is Part 5 of Title 24 and is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code. According to the City of Dana Point, permits are generally required for almost all construction. Your association may also have its own rules for exterior work.

Your water bill shows who serves your address. A leak on the street side of the meter is generally the utility's concern, and a leak on the house side is yours.

Monarch Beach sits just down the coast and has its own page: see Monarch Beach plumbing. Water heaters are a common call in both, and water heater repair explains how that visit works.
