Housing era first

San Clemente homes by era, and what each tends to need

Ole Hanson, a former Seattle mayor, bought the land in 1925 and laid out San Clemente as a planned Spanish-style town, and the city was incorporated in 1928. That date matters to a plumber, because the oldest parts of town carry the oldest pipes.

The Census counted 26,647 housing units in 2020, which means a lot of different ages of plumbing share one small coastal city. The table shows how era usually maps to the calls we hear.

Why older San Clemente plumbing fails the way it does

Hanson's original deeds asked for red tile roofs and white exteriors, but the rule did not last, so the oldest streets have mixed styles and mixed repairs. A house that was built in the late 1920s and remodeled in the 1970s and again in the 2000s may have three generations of pipe behind one wall.

That mix is why a leak in an older San Clemente home is often found by tools rather than by guesswork. Leak detection uses acoustic and thermal methods to trace a line without opening the wrong wall, and a drain cleaning visit often starts with a camera when the home is old.

Before we arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in San Clemente

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, and a few steps before the plumber arrives limit the damage.

Close the main

Turn the home's main valve clockwise until it stops. If you cannot find it, say so on the call.

Check the street side

A leak on the street side of the meter is generally the water provider's concern. The City of San Clemente runs a 24/7 utilities hotline at (949) 366-1553 for water leaks and sewer overflows.

Stay clear of wet power

Keep away from wet outlets and appliances. Use the breaker only if you can reach it dry.

Photograph first

Take photos of the damage before cleanup. Your insurer or landlord will likely ask.

Local plumbing

San Clemente water, sewer and what the city says about it

The City of San Clemente's Utilities Division provides water service in the city. Its own water information page says most of the city's supply is imported through two systems that originate at Metropolitan, and that the city also operates two groundwater wells. The same page says the city completed a lead service line inventory and found no lead or galvanized service lines requiring replacement.

That finding is about service lines. The pipes inside a house are a separate matter. A home from the 1920s can still have galvanized interior pipe when the service line is fine, so low pressure or rusty water inside an older house is worth a look. If those lines are failing, repiping is the lasting answer.

The Orange County Local Agency Formation Commission lists parts of San Clemente as served by South Coast Water District, the same district that supplies Dana Point. If you are not sure which utility serves your address, check your water bill before you call about a meter or a street-side leak.

What San Clemente calls about

The services San Clemente homes need most

Many calls begin with a symptom instead of a service name: a slow drain, a cold shower, a stain on the ceiling. These are the services they usually lead to.

Clogs and backups lead to drain cleaning and, for repeat blockages, to hydro jetting. A leaking or cold heater leads to water heater repair or replacement. A meter that will not stop leads to leak detection, and a main line that has failed leads to sewer line repair.
