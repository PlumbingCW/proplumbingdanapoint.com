What to do when a pipe bursts

These steps stop most of the damage. Do them in order, and call as soon as the water is stopped.

  1. 1

    Close the main valve

    Turn the main shutoff clockwise until it stops. It is usually near the meter or where the line enters the house.

  2. 2

    Open a low faucet

    Drain the line by opening the lowest tap, which relieves pressure and removes standing water.

  3. 3

    Mind the power

    Keep away from wet outlets and appliances, and switch off breakers for the flooded area only if you can reach them dry.

  4. 4

    Call and photograph

    Call the 24/7 line, then photograph the damage before moving anything.

Why pipes burst

What fails, and what that tells the plumber

A burst is the end of a slower process. Copper corrodes from the inside and develops pinholes that grow. Galvanized steel rusts shut from the inside and fails at threaded joints. Plastic fittings crack at stress points. Water hammer and pressure spikes add stress to joints that are already weak.

The plumber looks at what failed and what surrounds it. If the failed section is isolated, a replacement section solves it. If the pipe is thin everywhere, a burst in one spot will be followed by another, and that conversation moves toward repiping.

Other failures

Cracked pipe, supply valve and connector failures

Not every flood involves a burst main line. These are the other common culprits that also need fast attention.

Supply valve failure

A shutoff valve under a sink or behind a toilet can crack or leak at the stem. Shut the main and replace the valve.

Braided connector

The flexible line to a toilet, faucet or washing machine can split at the crimp. Replace them every few years.

Copper joint failure

A soldered joint can let go from age or stress. A new joint or a short section fixes it.

Cracked drain pipe

Drain lines crack too. A cracked drain leaks only when water runs, which makes it harder to spot.

Local plumbing

Why coastal homes see pinholes before bursts

Frozen pipes are not the usual culprit on the Orange County coast. Here, corrosion is more common than freezing, and salt air makes it worse on exposed fittings, hose bibs and garage plumbing. Older copper and galvanized lines in Capistrano Beach and Lantern Village homes, where building began in 1925, have had a long time to thin.

A pinhole that sprays a fine mist inside a wall can do as much damage as a split pipe, and it often goes unnoticed for weeks. If you find one, ask the plumber to check the pipe around it. Corrosion is rarely limited to a single spot.

Pipe materials

What replacement pipe usually looks like

The material used for the repair matters as much as the repair itself, and a plumber should tell you what they are installing.

Copper

  • Long record, solid in most conditions
  • Needs soldered or press joints
  • Can corrode in some water conditions
  • Higher material cost

PEX

  • Flexible, fewer joints, quick to install
  • Resists corrosion and pinholes
  • Must be protected from sunlight
  • Common choice for repipes

A repair that mixes materials needs proper transition fittings. If a large part of the house is failing, a full repipe may cost less than several repairs.

Codes and sources

What code and which office apply

Replacement pipe in a burst pipe repair has to meet the California Plumbing Code, which is Part 5 of Title 24 and is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) published by IAPMO. The City of Dana Point's Building and Safety Division, at (949) 248-3564, says permits are generally required for almost all construction, so ask whether a larger pipe replacement needs one.
