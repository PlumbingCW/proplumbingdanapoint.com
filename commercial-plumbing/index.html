Leaks

Commercial leak detection

Commercial buildings have longer pipe runs and more fixtures than a house, so leaks can hide a long way from where they show. A leak detection visit uses the same methods as residential work, including acoustic listening, thermal imaging and pressure testing, and often takes longer.

A tenant or a property manager can describe where water shows up and when, which narrows the search. See leak detection for the methods in detail, and keep records of when the problem began.

Maintenance

Grease traps, backflow testing and storm drains

Three recurring commercial jobs sit inside this service. Grease traps and interceptors catch fat from kitchens before it reaches the sewer, and they need to be pumped and cleaned regularly or the kitchen drain backs up. Backflow preventers protect the public water supply, and many water districts require annual testing of certain devices. Ask South Coast Water District whether yours is on its list.

Storm drains and area drains on a commercial lot clear rainwater, and debris and sediment block them. Cleaning them before the rainy season prevents flooding in a parking lot or a loading dock. A site that has all three on a schedule has fewer emergencies.

Local plumbing

Harbor shops, Pacific Coast Highway businesses and older buildings

Dana Point Harbor, dedicated on July 31, 1971, has marina shops and restaurants, and the businesses on Pacific Coast Highway add more. Older commercial buildings may have aging drains and grease lines that were sized for a different use.

A restaurant that changes concept or adds a kitchen line may find that its existing drains and grease capacity no longer match. A plumber who looks at the lines before the change avoids a closure after it. For repeated kitchen drain clogs, hydro jetting clears the buildup on a schedule.

Decision

Reactive calls or scheduled maintenance

Businesses fall into one of two patterns.

Reactive

  • Call when something fails
  • No scheduled cost
  • Downtime is unplanned
  • Emergencies cost more in lost business

Scheduled

  • Regular cleaning and testing
  • Predictable cost
  • Fewer after-hours calls
  • Easier to document for inspectors and landlords

Commercial plumbing sometimes calls for specialty trades. Fire sprinkler systems and medical gas are specialty trades with their own licensing, and the dispatcher will tell you honestly if a job falls outside what a general plumber handles.

Codes and sources

What code and which office apply

Commercial plumbing follows the California Plumbing Code, which is Part 5 of Title 24 and is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Grease interceptors, backflow devices and fixture counts for a business are code items, and the City of Dana Point's Building and Safety Division at (949) 248-3564 is the office to ask about permits.
