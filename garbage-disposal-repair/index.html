Disposal problems that need a fast call

Most disposal problems are annoying. A few cause water damage.

Jam and hum

Why a disposal hums but does not spin

A humming disposal has power but cannot turn. That usually means a jam.

A foreign object

A bone, a utensil or a piece of fruit pit can wedge between the impellers and the shredder ring.

Tripped reset

Most disposals have a red reset button on the bottom. Pressing it may restore the unit after a jam clears.

Worn motor

A motor that hums and will not turn after clearing the jam may have failed.

Seized flywheel

Corrosion can lock the flywheel, particularly on older units.

Double sinks

Plumbing a double sink with a disposal

A double kitchen sink has two basins that share one drain. The disposal usually sits under one, and a tailpiece connects the other basin to the same trap. A dishwasher connects to the disposal inlet or to an air gap.

Problems often trace to one of these connections. A loose slip nut under the second basin, a cracked tailpiece or a dishwasher hose that has slipped off the disposal inlet can all produce a leak. A plumber can reconnect the lines, replace the parts and test the whole assembly.

Decision

Repair or replace a disposal

Disposals are inexpensive enough that replacement is often the better choice.

Repair when

  • A jam can be cleared and the motor is fine
  • A leak is at a seal or a connection
  • The unit is fairly new
  • The unit is a good model

Replace when

  • The housing leaks
  • The motor is burned out
  • The unit is over ten years old
  • It jams repeatedly

A new disposal needs an electrical outlet or hardwired connection, a mounting ring and a drain connection. The plumber handles all of it.

Local plumbing

Kitchens, harbor restaurants and the line behind the disposal

Home kitchens in Dana Point share a common failure point with the town's restaurants: grease. Cooking oil and fat poured down the sink coat the pipe beyond the disposal, and the disposal itself cannot stop it. If a disposal repair is followed by another clog, the problem is probably the drain line, and hydro jetting may be the cleaner fix.

Older homes in Capistrano Beach and Lantern Village may have kitchen drains that run a long way under the house to reach the main. A longer run gives grease more place to settle. A camera look and a cleaning together settle the question.

Codes and sources

What code and which office apply

A disposal's drain connection and trap are covered by the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The electrical side, including the switch and the cord or hardwire connection, follows the California Electrical Code, so a plumber who finds a wiring fault will say so and stop.
