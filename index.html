What counts as a plumbing emergency

Call the 24/7 line for any of these

Not every plumbing problem needs a midnight visit, but these six do not wait for morning. If you are unsure which one you have, call anyway and describe what you see.

  • ✓
    Burst or leaking pipe. Water you cannot stop is the clearest emergency. Close the main valve and call for burst pipe repair before the ceiling or floor takes on more water.
  • ✓
    Sewer backup. If sewage is rising in a tub, shower or floor drain, stop using every fixture. Sewer line cleaning clears the main so the house can drain again.
  • ✓
    No hot water or a leaking tank. A tank that is wet at the base or a heater that has gone cold calls for water heater repair or, on an old unit, water heater replacement.
  • ✓
    Overflowing toilet or a clog you cannot clear. Plunging that does not work usually means the blockage sits farther down. Drain cleaning reaches it with a cable or a jetter.
  • ✓
    A leak you cannot see. A meter that keeps turning with everything off, a warm patch on the floor or a sudden high bill points to a hidden line. Leak detection finds it without opening walls blindly.
  • ✓
    A smell of gas. Leave the building first. SoCalGas tells customers to evacuate, then call 1-800-427-2200, or 911 if anyone is in danger. Do not call a plumber from inside the house.
Before we arrive

Four things to do while you wait

Limit the damage in the first ten minutes

These steps cost nothing and often decide how much repair work follows. Do them in order, then keep your phone nearby in case the plumber calls with a question.

Find the main shutoff

Most homes have a main valve near the water meter or where the line enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Stop using drains

Do not flush, run the dishwasher or start laundry during a backup. Every gallon you add has nowhere to go.

Mind the electricity

Keep people away from wet outlets, power strips and low appliances. Switch off the breaker for a flooded area only if you can reach it dry.

Take photos first

Photograph the damage before you mop up. Your insurer or landlord may ask for them, and they help the plumber too.

Local plumbing

Why Dana Point plumbing has its own habits

Dana Point became a city on January 1, 1989, but its plumbing is much older than that. Lantern Village, the historic core near the harbor, holds some of the oldest houses in town, and Capistrano Beach began building out in 1925. Homes from that era often still have original drain lines. Those lines crack and shift, so a sewer camera inspection before you dig is cheaper than guessing.

Homes around the Headlands and Niguel Shores sit close to coastal bluffs, and bluff erosion is a documented issue in Niguel Shores. A leak under a sloping yard can surface far from where it starts, which is why acoustic leak detection beats opening ground at random. Monarch Beach and Dana Hills were built out well after the 1920s, so the likelier calls there involve worn water heaters, softeners, disposals and valves rather than original drain lines.

Salt air also does quiet damage. Exterior hose bibs, garage water heater fittings and shutoff valves near the coast corrode faster than the same parts inland. If a valve is green, crusty or stuck, replace it before the day you need it to close.
