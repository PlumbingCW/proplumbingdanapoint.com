How to Repair a Leaky Outside Faucet: Dana Point, CA Homeowner Guide

An outside faucet, also called a hose bib or spigot, takes more abuse than any other fixture in the house. It sits in the weather, it is turned by hand with a hose attached and on the coast it breathes salt air all year.

Most leaks are small and fixable in ten minutes. A few are not. This guide shows how to tell which you have, and if water is coming through a wall, shut the main valve and read emergency faucet repair.

Locate the leak

Where the water comes from tells you the fix

Turn the faucet on and off while you watch. The place the water appears is the clue.

Outside faucet leak locations

DIY steps

Fixing a leak at the handle

A leak around the handle is the easiest repair. You need an adjustable wrench and a small packing kit or a length of graphite packing.

  • ✓
    Shut off water to the spigot. Use the interior shutoff if there is one, or close the main valve.
  • ✓
    Open the faucet. Drain what is left in the pipe.
  • ✓
    Tighten the packing nut. A quarter turn often stops a weep at the stem.
  • ✓
    If it still leaks, remove the handle and nut. Unscrew the handle, then the packing nut, and replace the old packing or O-ring.
  • ✓
    Reassemble and test. Open the water slowly and check for drips.

Spout leaks

When the faucet drips from the spout

A drip from the spout when the handle is closed means the washer or seat inside has worn. Many outdoor faucets are compression faucets, which seal with a rubber washer on the end of the stem. Replacing the washer, and the screw that holds it, is a short job once the stem is out.

If the seat inside the faucet body is pitted, a new washer will not seal for long. A plumber can dress the seat or replace the whole faucet. Replacing the faucet is often the better choice if the body is also corroded.

Frost-proof sillcocks

Long-stem faucets and what can fail inside the wall

Many outdoor faucets are long-stem frost-proof types. The valve seat is several inches inside the wall, where it stays above freezing. That design protects against frozen pipes, but it also means a leak inside the wall can go unseen. If the faucet drips only when a hose is attached and the water then runs inside the wall or the crawlspace, the stem seal has likely failed.

A hose left attached in cold weather can trap water in the stem. On the Orange County coast hard freezes are uncommon, so the more usual failures are wear and corrosion. Either way, a faucet that runs water inside the wall is a job for a plumber.

Dana Point note

Salt air, recycled water and the tap you are about to use

South Coast Water District, which supplies Dana Point, also provides recycled water. The district has delivered recycled water since 1982. That is a reason to be careful about any tap or valve you did not install. A tap marked as recycled or non-potable is for irrigation only, and should never fill a drinking bowl, a pool or a hose used to rinse food.

Salt air is the other local factor. It corrodes brass and steel, so an outdoor faucet within sight of the ocean ages faster than one a few miles inland. A green crust at the stem and a handle that no longer turns easily are warning signs. Replace the faucet before it fails on a day you need it.

Decision

Repair or replace the faucet

A small leak is worth repairing. A corroded body is not.

Repair when

  • The leak is at the packing or a washer
  • The body is sound
  • The handle turns smoothly
  • The faucet is not decades old

Replace when

  • The body is cracked or pitted
  • The stem is seized
  • Water runs inside the wall
  • Parts are unavailable

If a leak keeps returning at the same spot, check the supply line. A section of pipe behind the faucet can fail, and then leak detection helps locate the leak before anyone opens a wall.

Preventive

Habits that make an outside faucet last

A few habits help a spigot last.

  • ✓
    Disconnect hoses when not in use. A hose left on holds pressure on the stem and traps water.
  • ✓
    Open and close it slowly. Forcing it strips the stem and wears the seat.
  • ✓
    Check it every few months. Look for green crust and a handle that turns stiffly.
  • ✓
    Know the shutoff. Find the interior valve for the outdoor line, and the main valve, before you need them.
Tools

What you need before you start

Gather an adjustable wrench, a screwdriver, packing or a replacement washer, thread seal tape and a rag. Take a photo of the faucet so you can match the parts at the store. If the hardware store has no matching stem, the faucet is a candidate for replacement.

Do the work in daylight with the water off, and keep the hose disconnected.

Codes and sources

What code and which office apply

Outdoor faucets follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including backflow prevention. A hose bib should have a vacuum breaker, according to the code, so a hose cannot pull dirty water back into the house.
