Outdoor Tankless Water Heater Installation: A Dana Point, CA Homeowner Guide

An outdoor tankless water heater mounts on an exterior wall and heats water on demand. It frees the garage or closet, and it vents straight to the open air. On the Dana Point coast it also faces salt air and sun, which a unit in a closet never sees.

This guide covers where the unit can go, what it needs and what to ask. For service after installation, see tankless water heater service.

Why outdoors

What an outdoor tankless install gets you

The appeal is simple. The unit sits outside, so there is no flue through the roof and no combustion air to arrange inside. It frees the space where the old tank stood, and a short run to the kitchen or bath shortens the wait for hot water.

The trade-off is exposure. Rain, wind, sun, dust and salt all reach the unit and its connections. Models rated for outdoor use are built to handle that, but fittings, wiring and the vent cap still need protection.

Siting

Where an outdoor unit can go

A good location balances access, clearances and the length of pipe to the fixtures.

Clearances

Manufacturers specify distances from windows, doors, vents and eaves. Follow the manual, and let the plumber check them on site.

Gas and electric access

The unit needs a gas line and an outlet or circuit. A short run keeps cost down.

Distance to fixtures

A shorter pipe run to the bathrooms means less waiting for hot water.

Service access

Leave room to open the front panel and reach the filters for flushing.

Salt air

What coastal air does to an outdoor unit

Salt in the air settles on metal and speeds corrosion on fittings, screws and exposed connections. A unit a block from the water ages faster than one inland. The plumber can choose stainless or coated fittings, keep the vent cap clear and plan to inspect connections once a year.

Wiping the cabinet and checking for green crust on copper fittings takes a few minutes. Catching corrosion early is the difference between replacing a fitting and replacing a unit.

Gas and venting

Gas line size and the vent path

Tankless units draw a lot of fuel at once, so the gas line has to be large enough to feed the unit when it fires at full rate. A line that suited a tank may be too small. The plumber calculates the demand and tells you whether the line needs upgrading.

An outdoor unit usually has its own built-in vent, which avoids the long runs of special pipe that indoor units need. The cap still has to point away from windows, and the unit needs clear air all around it. If you also have gas appliances nearby, ask the plumber to check their clearances too.

Dana Point note

Bluff-top lots and outdoor equipment

The City of Dana Point's Building and Safety homeowner page says that specific areas, such as bluff-top lots or flood plain properties, may have additional requirements for work that is otherwise exempt from permits. Dana Point has coastal bluffs along the Headlands and in Niguel Shores, so a homeowner there should ask the division before mounting equipment on an exterior wall or yard.

The division's main number is (949) 248-3564. Contractors working in the city must hold a valid California contractor's license in good standing, and a licensed contractor can obtain any required permit.

Upkeep

A simple maintenance routine for an outdoor unit

An outdoor unit rewards a small routine. Once a quarter, look at the cabinet, the vent cap and the connections for corrosion, insects and debris. Wipe off salt film with a damp cloth, and clear leaves or webs from air intakes.

Once a year, flush the heat exchanger with a descaling solution and clean the inlet filter, according to the manufacturer's instructions. Keep the invoice, because some warranties ask for it. A unit that gets this attention will stay efficient, and small problems stay small.

Checklist

Questions to ask before you buy

These questions cover most of what goes wrong with outdoor installs.

  • ✓
    Is the model rated for outdoor use? Not every tankless unit is. Check the label.
  • ✓
    What will protect the connections? Ask about insulation, coatings and fittings suited to salt air.
  • ✓
    Is the gas line big enough? Ask for the demand calculation, not just a yes.
  • ✓
    Who pulls the permit? If one is needed, ask who handles it and when the inspection happens.
  • ✓
    How will it be flushed? Ask where the service valves go so flushing is easy.
Before the visit

What to photograph before the plumber arrives

Photograph the wall where the unit will hang, the gas meter, the water shutoff and the nearest electrical outlet. Measure the distance from the meter to the wall. These details let the plumber estimate the gas line run and the venting clearance without a second trip, and they make the quote more accurate.
