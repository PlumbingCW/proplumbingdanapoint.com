PEX

Installing PEX plumbing

PEX is flexible plastic pipe used for water supply. It runs through walls in long continuous lengths with fewer joints than copper, resists corrosion and takes less time to install. It is not used for drains or gas, and it must be kept out of sunlight.

A PEX installation uses specific fittings and tools. Crimp rings, clamp rings and expansion fittings are the common methods, and each needs the matching tool. A poorly made connection fails later, which is the reason to use a plumber who does it every day. The plumber should also protect the pipe where it passes through framing.

Bathrooms

Plumbing for a new or remodeled bathroom

A bathroom needs hot and cold supply for each fixture, drains sized for each, and a vent so the drains work. The order matters: the plumbing rough-in comes before walls are closed and floors are tiled, and the work is inspected before it is hidden.

The City of Dana Point's Building and Safety Division says permits are generally required for almost all construction, including bathroom and kitchen renovations. A licensed contractor can obtain the permit, and the plumber should tell you who pulls it and when the inspection happens.

Gas

Gas lines: safety first

Gas work is regulated and unforgiving. If you smell gas, do not call a plumber first. SoCalGas tells customers to evacuate immediately and call 1-800-427-2200 from a safe location, and to call 911 if there is danger to life or if SoCalGas cannot be reached.

For a planned job, such as a new range connection, a gas dryer or a barbecue line, the plumber installs and tests the line. A pressure test confirms there are no leaks before the appliance is connected. If you are not sure whether a smell is gas, treat it as gas and leave.

Decision

New pipes: PEX or copper

Both are acceptable, and the right choice depends on the project.

PEX

  • Flexible and fast
  • Fewer joints
  • Resists corrosion and scale
  • Not for sunlight or gas

Copper

  • Rigid and durable
  • Soldered or pressed joints
  • Handles heat and sun
  • Higher material and labor cost

If an old house needs its whole supply replaced rather than one new run, see repiping. For fixtures that already exist and just need fixing, see faucet repair.

What code and which office apply

New piping is governed by the California Plumbing Code, which is Part 5 of Title 24 of the California Code of Regulations and is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
