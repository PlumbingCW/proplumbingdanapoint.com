Repiping in Dana Point, CA

Repiping replaces the water supply lines in a house, either all of them or the failing sections. It is the sensible answer when pipes keep springing leaks, water runs brown, or pressure falls despite new fixtures.

It is a planned service, so there is no emergency surcharge for a scheduled visit, though the 24/7 line is open if a failing pipe floods the house tonight. You get a clear price before work starts, and the plumber explains each choice.

Signs

Signs a house needs repiping

One leak does not make a repipe. A pattern does. Look for these signs together.

  • ✓
    Repeated pinhole leaks. Two or three leaks in a year, in different places, mean the pipe is thin throughout.
  • ✓
    Brown or rusty water. Rust from galvanized pipe discolors the water, especially after it sat overnight.
  • ✓
    Low pressure at every faucet. Mineral and rust buildup narrows the inside of old pipe.
  • ✓
    Visible corrosion. Green crust on copper joints or flaking on galvanized pipe at the water heater.
  • ✓
    An aging house with original lines. Houses built decades ago may still have their first supply pipes.
Old pipe

Galvanized, copper and the pipes behind your walls

Homes built before the 1960s often have galvanized steel supply lines. Galvanized pipe rusts from the inside, so the interior narrows even when the outside looks fine. That is what causes weak pressure and discolored water. Copper replaced galvanized steel in most homes, and it lasts a long time, but it can corrode and develop pinholes in some water and soil conditions.

A plumber can tell what you have by looking at exposed pipe near the water heater and under sinks. Galvanized looks gray and threaded. Copper is reddish-brown or green with soldered joints. Newer pipe may be PEX, which is flexible and usually colored red, blue or white.

Materials

Copper or PEX for a repipe in Dana Point?

Both are accepted choices, and the right one depends on the house and your budget.

Copper

  • Long track record
  • Solid in most conditions
  • Needs soldered or pressed joints
  • More labor, higher cost

PEX

  • Flexible, runs through walls with fewer cuts
  • Resists pinholes and scale
  • Quicker to install, usually less wall damage
  • Must be kept out of sunlight

Ask the plumber which they recommend for your house and why, and whether the plan reaches the water heater, the shutoff valves and the hose bibs.

Local plumbing

Older Dana Point houses and what repiping involves

Capistrano Beach began building out in 1925 and Lantern Village near the harbor holds some of the oldest homes in Dana Point. A house from that era may have had its pipes replaced once, or not at all. The plumber can tell you what is in place and how much of it is original.

Many Dana Point homes are close to the coast. Salt air corrodes exterior fittings and hose bibs, and some water conditions speed pinholes in copper. If you are repiping, ask the plumber to replace the shutoff valves at the same time, since an old valve that will not close is a problem you will discover at the worst moment.

A repipe usually needs a permit. The Dana Point Building and Safety Division can say what applies to your house, and a licensed contractor can obtain it. You can reach them at (949) 248-3564.

Pricing

What affects the price of repiping

Repiping is one of the larger plumbing projects in a home, and the price varies with the house more than almost any other service.

House size and layoutMore fixtures and more distance mean more pipe.
Pipe materialCopper and PEX cost different amounts in parts and labor.
Wall and ceiling accessFinished walls, tile and stucco affect labor and patching.
Number of fixturesEach added bathroom, hose bib and appliance connection is more work.
Permits and inspectionFees and timing vary, and the plumber should explain them.

Homeowner insurance generally does not pay to replace pipes that are worn out. It may cover the damage if a sudden failure floods the house. Ask your insurer what applies.

Codes and sources

What code and which office apply

A repipe follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) published by IAPMO. The City of Dana Point's Building and Safety Division, at (949) 248-3564, says permits are generally required for almost all construction, and a repipe is the kind of job to ask about. Ask the plumber to name who pulls the permit and when the inspection happens.
