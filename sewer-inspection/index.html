What a sewer camera inspection shows

The camera sends live video to a screen, and the cable is marked with distance. The plumber can tell you what the problem is and exactly how many feet from the cleanout it sits. That distance matters because it turns a vague repair into a defined one.

  • ✓
    Cracks and offsets. Joints where two sections no longer line up, or a longitudinal crack along the pipe.
  • ✓
    Roots. Fine hairlike roots at the joints, or a thick mass that has narrowed the pipe.
  • ✓
    Bellies. A low spot where water pools and solids settle.
  • ✓
    Buildup. Grease and scale on the wall that a cleaning will remove.
  • ✓
    Pipe material. Clay, cast iron, ABS, PVC or fiber, each with its own failure pattern.
Buying a home

Inspecting a sewer line before closing

A standard home inspection looks at fixtures and visible plumbing. It does not look inside a buried sewer line. A camera inspection does, and the cost is small compared with the cost of a collapsed line found after you move in.

Ask for the video and a written note of what it showed, including the distance to each problem. If the camera finds damage, you have information for the purchase negotiation. If it finds a clean line, you have the first piece of a maintenance record.

Reasons to look

When a camera look is worth the visit

Some homeowners wait for a backup. Others look earlier, and a few of these situations are good reasons to do so.

Repeat slow drains

A line that keeps slowing despite cleaning needs an inside view.

A tree near the line

Roots seek water, and a mature tree within reach of the line is the usual source.

A purchase or listing

Buyers and sellers both benefit from a documented pipe condition.

Before a landscape project

Hardscape, pools and new plantings go over buried pipes. Know where yours runs first.

Local plumbing

Why the sewer in an older Dana Point house deserves a look

Houses in Capistrano Beach, where building began in 1925, and in Lantern Village near the harbor can be a century old or close to it. Pipe laid then was built to the standards of its day, not ours. A camera is the only way to know whether the original line is still sound or has been replaced in sections over the years.

On the Headlands and in Niguel Shores, homes sit close to coastal bluffs, and ground movement can shift pipe joints over time. Where that is a concern, ask the plumber to note any offsets and their distance from the house so a future owner can compare.
