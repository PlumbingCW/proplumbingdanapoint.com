Emergency Slab Leak Repair in Dana Point, CA

A slab leak is a leak in a pipe that runs under or inside a concrete foundation. If your home sits on a slab, the first signs are usually a floor that feels warm, water bubbling up under flooring or a water meter that keeps turning with everything shut off.

Emergency slab leak repair in Dana Point starts with the 24/7 line. Close the main water valve, stay off wet flooring and call. A plumber can locate the leak and explain whether a spot repair or a reroute makes more sense.

What it looks like

Signs of a slab leak you can check yourself

Slab leaks hide, but they leave a pattern. Check these before the plumber arrives.

  • ✓
    A warm spot on the floor. A leaking hot water line heats the concrete above it. Barefoot, you can often feel it.
  • ✓
    Water appearing at a wall base or under flooring. Moisture that rises through a slab lifts baseboards, buckles wood and stains carpet.
  • ✓
    The sound of running water in a quiet house. A hiss or trickle with every fixture off is a strong clue.
  • ✓
    A meter that moves with the main valve open and all water off. The standard test, covered under leak detection.
  • ✓
    Unexplained mold or a musty smell at floor level. Constant moisture from below feeds both.
Urgent

When a slab leak is an emergency

A slab leak is urgent when water is coming up into the living space, because wet flooring and drywall begin to fail within a day or two. It is also urgent when the leak is on a hot water line, since that can warm the slab and raise the bill quickly.

While you wait for the plumber, shut off the main valve, open a faucet at the lowest point to relieve pressure, move furniture and rugs off the wet area and photograph the damage for your insurer. If the water heater is nearby, turn it off to protect the tank and the elements.

Repair choices

Spot repair, reroute or repipe?

Once the leak is located, there are usually three ways forward. The plumber should explain which fits and why.

Break the slab and repair

  • Cuts a hole in the concrete directly over the leak
  • Fixes the pipe where it failed
  • Makes sense for a single isolated leak on otherwise sound pipe
  • Leaves a patch in the floor that needs finishing

Reroute the line

  • Abandons the failed pipe and runs a new one through walls or attic
  • Avoids opening the slab
  • Makes sense when the pipe has failed in several places
  • May need access to finished walls and ceilings

If the line keeps failing in different places, the sensible fix may be repiping the whole house, which replaces the problem instead of chasing it.

Why slab pipes leak

What typically causes under-slab leaks

Pipes under a slab fail for a few repeatable reasons.

Corrosion

Copper in some soils and water conditions thins over decades and develops pinholes.

Abrasion

Pipes rub against concrete or gravel as the house heats and cools, wearing through the wall.

Soil movement

Ground shifts stress joints and fittings below the slab.

Poor original installation

Kinks, tight bends and stressed joints fail earlier than clean runs.

Local plumbing

Slab homes and coastal soil in Dana Point

Not every Dana Point home is built on a slab. Many older houses in Capistrano Beach and Lantern Village were built on raised foundations, and some newer homes were built on slabs. Check your own house, or ask the dispatcher, before assuming a slab leak is the problem.

For homes that are on a slab, the coastal setting matters. Soil movement near bluffs, and corrosion in pipes near salty ground, can both stress buried supply lines.
