Overflow

What to do when a toilet overflows

An overflow is stoppable if you act in the first minute. Do not flush again.

  1. 1

    Stop the water

    Turn the valve behind or beside the toilet clockwise. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and hold the float up.

  2. 2

    Contain it

    Put towels around the base and move rugs and electronics out of the way.

  3. 3

    Stop all drains

    If other fixtures gurgle, the blockage is in a shared line. Do not run water.

  4. 4

    Call

    Tell the dispatcher whether it is only this toilet or several fixtures.

Common problems

Running, leaking and rocking toilets

Toilet repairs fall into a few categories, and most are straightforward once the cause is clear.

Running toilet

A worn flapper, a corroded fill valve or a chain that is too long lets water leak into the bowl. The meter keeps turning and the bill rises.

Leak at the base

A failed wax ring or a cracked flange lets water escape when you flush. Water at the floor is a sign.

Rocking bowl

A loose bowl moves on its seal. It needs tightening or a new ring before the seal fails.

Weak flush

A clogged rim jet, a low tank water level or a partial blockage reduces flush power.

At the base

Wax rings, flanges and what lies under the toilet

A toilet sits on a flange, a ring fixed to the floor that connects the bowl to the drain. A wax ring or a foam gasket makes the seal between them. When the seal fails, water leaks at the base, and sewer gas can enter the room.

A wax ring is inexpensive, but the floor under it can be damaged if the leak has gone on a while. The plumber should check the subfloor for softness. A cracked or corroded flange needs a repair ring or replacement, since a new wax ring on a broken flange will fail again.

Decision

Repair the toilet or replace it?

Most toilets are worth repairing. Some are not.

Repair makes sense when

  • The toilet is a standard design in good condition
  • Only the flapper, valve or seal has failed
  • The bowl and tank are not cracked
  • A part for it is easy to find

Replace when

  • The porcelain is cracked
  • It uses more water than modern models need
  • Repairs are frequent
  • Parts are no longer made

Many homeowners replace a toilet during a remodel. The Dana Point Building and Safety Division says permits are generally required for bathroom renovations, so ask the plumber whether yours needs one.

Local plumbing

Older toilets and older drains in Dana Point

Homes in Capistrano Beach and Lantern Village date to the 1920s, and many have had their bathrooms remodeled more than once. A newer toilet on an old cast iron or clay drain can still clog repeatedly if the line has roots or a belly. If a new toilet clogs often, the problem is probably the line, and drain cleaning or a camera look is the next step.

Another point about older houses is the shutoff valves. A valve that has not been turned in twenty years often will not close, or leaks when you try. Replace the shutoff at the same time as the toilet repair, and replace the braided supply line, which has a limited life.
