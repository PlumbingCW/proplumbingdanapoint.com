What an install includes

The parts of a proper installation

A water heater is more than a tank on a stand. A complete installation covers several pieces, and a quote should list each one.

  • ✓
    The unit itself. Matched to household size and fuel type.
  • ✓
    Venting. A gas heater needs a vent that carries exhaust outside, sized and sloped correctly.
  • ✓
    Expansion tank. A closed system can build pressure when water heats. An expansion tank absorbs it and protects fittings.
  • ✓
    Pressure and temperature relief valve and discharge line. A safety device that must be piped to a safe discharge point.
  • ✓
    Strapping. Required in California to hold the heater against earthquake motion.
  • ✓
    Drain pan or other leak protection. Where a leak would damage a floor, a pan with a drain protects it.
Sizing

Choosing the right size

A tank that is too small runs out. One that is too large wastes energy. The usual guide is the number of people in the house and the fixtures that run at the same time, such as two showers and a dishwasher.

Tankless units are sized differently, by flow rate and temperature rise, not by tank volume. A plumber can calculate it from the fixtures you use, and should explain why the number is what it is.

Vent pipes

Water heater vent pipe installation

A gas heater makes exhaust gases that must leave the house. The vent pipe has to rise at the right angle, keep clear of combustibles and end at a proper termination. A vent that is undersized, sagging or disconnected can send carbon monoxide into the living space.

That is why venting is among the first things a plumber checks. If the existing vent is rusted, loose or poorly pitched, replacing it is part of a sound installation. Ask to see the vent during the visit.

Options

Gas, electric or heat pump

Fuel and type shape the cost of installation and the cost of running the heater.

Gas tank or tankless

  • Fast recovery
  • Needs a vent and a gas line of the right size
  • Common in older homes with existing gas
  • Tankless gas often needs a larger line

Electric or heat pump

  • No vent for a standard electric tank
  • Heat pump models use much less electricity
  • Needs adequate electrical capacity
  • Heat pump needs space and airflow

Local plumbing

Permits and code in Dana Point

Dana Point's Building and Safety Division issues plumbing permits and performs inspections. Its homeowner page says permits are generally required for almost all construction, and contractors working in the city must hold a valid California contractor's license in good standing. The page does not address water heaters specifically, so call the division at (949) 248-3564 to ask whether your installation needs a permit.

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped. The plumber should include the straps in the quote and install them at the same time. Ask to see them when the job is done.

Checklist

Before installation day

A little preparation avoids delays.

  • ✓
    Clear the area. Move stored items away from the heater and the path to it.
  • ✓
    Know where the shutoffs are. The plumber will need the main water valve, and the gas or breaker for the old heater.
  • ✓
    Decide on the type. Settling tank or tankless, and gas or electric, before the visit saves a return trip.
