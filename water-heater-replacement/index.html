First steps

What to do the moment a tank fails

A failed tank can release a full tank of water within minutes. These steps limit the damage.

  1. 1

    Close the cold supply

    Turn the valve on the pipe above the heater clockwise until it stops.

  2. 2

    Cut the power or gas

    Turn off the breaker for an electric unit or the gas control for a gas unit.

  3. 3

    Drain what you can

    Attach a hose to the drain valve if you can reach it safely, and run it outside or to a floor drain.

  4. 4

    Call and photograph

    Call the 24/7 line and photograph the damage for your insurer.

Choosing

Replacing like for like, or changing the type

A replacement is a chance to change the system. Most homeowners choose a like-for-like tank because it is the simplest path. Others upgrade to a larger tank, a tankless unit or a heat pump (hybrid) heater, which saves energy but costs more up front.

If you are thinking about replacing a gas heater with a heat pump model, tell the dispatcher before the visit. It changes the electrical work and the space needed, and it is not a decision to make while standing in a wet garage. The plumber can give you a clear comparison, and you can also read about tankless water heaters on their own page.

Decision

Tank or tankless: what changes

The choice depends on household size, space and budget. The plumber should compare them for your house.

Tank heater

  • Lower cost to buy and install
  • Stores hot water, so it can run out
  • Simple to repair and service
  • Shorter life than tankless

Tankless heater

  • Heats water on demand, so no running out
  • Takes less space
  • Higher cost, and may need larger gas lines or venting
  • Needs periodic flushing

A planned swap is covered under water heater installation, which goes into sizing, venting and the expansion tank.

Code

Strapping, permits and what the city says

Some of the work around a water heater is regulated. California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion, and the code applies to units up to 120 gallons that have a pre-engineered strapping kit available.

The City of Dana Point's Building and Safety Division issues plumbing permits. Its homeowner page says permits are generally required for almost all construction but does not address water heaters specifically, so ask the division directly at (949) 248-3564. A licensed contractor can obtain the permit, and contractors working in Dana Point must hold a valid California contractor's license in good standing.

Local plumbing

Replacing a heater in a Dana Point garage or closet

Water heaters in older Dana Point homes sit wherever the original builder put them, often in a garage, a laundry closet or a hall closet. Replacement is easier when the new unit fits the old footprint. A larger tank may need more headroom and a different vent path.

Homes in Capistrano Beach and Lantern Village, where building began in the 1920s, can have older gas lines, tight closets and vents that do not meet current practice. A plumber who looks at the vent and the gas connection before quoting avoids surprises on installation day. Newer homes in Monarch Beach and Dana Hills are more likely to have standard layouts.
