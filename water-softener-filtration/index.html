Water Softener and Filtration in Dana Point, CA

Hard water leaves white scale on faucets, spots on glasses and crusty buildup inside water heaters. A water softener removes the minerals that cause it, and a filter removes other things such as chlorine taste or sediment.

Water softener and filtration work in Dana Point covers installation, repair and valve service. This is a planned service, and the first step is knowing what is in your tap water, not guessing.

Test your tap before buying anything

A softener is sized and set from the hardness of the water, and a filter is chosen from what you want to remove. Guessing leads to the wrong equipment, so start with a test. A home test strip gives a rough number. A lab test gives a precise one.

You can also ask the water utility for its annual water quality report. South Coast Water District supplies Dana Point and says its report is available at scwd.org/report. We do not publish a hardness figure on this page, because the district's own report is the right source and the number can change.

Which system

Softener, filter or both

People often use the words as if they mean the same thing, and they do not.

  • ✓
    Water softener. Uses salt or potassium to swap hardness minerals for sodium or potassium. It protects pipes and appliances and reduces scale.
  • ✓
    Carbon filter. Removes chlorine taste and odor, and some other compounds, but does not soften.
  • ✓
    Sediment filter. Catches sand and particles before they reach fixtures and appliances.
  • ✓
    Reverse osmosis. A small under-sink system that polishes drinking water by pushing it through a fine membrane.
  • ✓
    Whole-house filtration. A filter at the main that treats all the water in the house.

Repair

Softener not working: common problems

Softeners are simple machines, and most failures are small. A call about a system that is not working usually traces to one of these.

Salt bridge

A hard crust in the brine tank stops salt from dissolving, so the unit regenerates without actually softening.

Control valve failure

The valve directs water during regeneration. A worn valve can leak, stick or lose its settings.

Resin that has worn out

After many years the resin beads stop working and need replacing.

Power or settings issue

A tripped outlet or a reset timer can stop regeneration.

Local plumbing

What to ask the district and what to watch at home

South Coast Water District says it provides water and wastewater service to about 35,000 residents. Its supply comes from imported water bought through the Municipal Water District of Orange County and from local groundwater, according to the district's regional planning documents. The mix can shift, so the report for the most recent year is the one to read.

At home, watch for white scale on shower doors and faucet tips, a crust inside kettles and spots on dishes. These suggest hard water. Also look at the water heater, because scale inside the tank shortens its life. If you see signs, a test and a plan are worth doing. See tankless water heater service for how scale affects on-demand units.

Decision

Salt softener or salt-free conditioner

Two types of treatment are common. They work differently.

Salt-based softener

  • Removes hardness minerals
  • Protects appliances and pipes
  • Needs salt refills and a drain connection
  • Adds a small amount of sodium or potassium

Salt-free conditioner

  • Alters scale so it does not stick
  • No salt and less maintenance
  • Does not remove hardness
  • Effectiveness varies by model

Some cities limit or regulate salt-based regeneration discharge. Ask the city or the plumber whether that applies where you live.
